# KaizenHacks Color Guidelines

## 1. Purpose

These guidelines define how the KaizenHacks color palette should be applied across brand materials.

The color system is not simply a collection of approved colors. It is a hierarchy.

The goal is to maintain a recognizable KaizenHacks identity while allowing different events, campaigns, platforms, and materials to have their own visual character.

---

# 2. Core Principle

**Deep Navy establishes the brand.  
Royal Blue provides energy.  
The lighter blues provide accents.  
Warm Paper and Off White provide breathing room.  
Ink provides structure and contrast.**

Do not treat every color as equally important.

---

# 3. Color Hierarchy

Use the following hierarchy:

### Primary

**Deep Navy — `#091F42`**

The default brand color.

### Secondary

**Royal Blue — `#254B85`**

The primary supporting brand color.

### Accent

**Mid Blue — `#3E70A5`**

Used for emphasis and interaction.

### Supporting

**Muted Blue — `#6499C1`**

Used for softer visual elements.

### Warm Neutral

**Warm Paper — `#CAC4BA`**

Used to introduce warmth and editorial character.

### Light Neutral

**Off White — `#F2F3F1`**

Used for light surfaces and negative space.

### Dark Neutral

**Ink — `#0D1014`**

Used primarily for text and deep neutral surfaces.

---

# 4. Primary Color Usage

Deep Navy should be the strongest visual representation of KaizenHacks.

Use it for:

- Major backgrounds
- Brand covers
- Headers
- Presentation covers
- Large sections
- Posters
- Primary graphic compositions

Avoid replacing Deep Navy with another color simply because the alternative is more visually exciting.

---

# 5. Secondary Color Usage

Royal Blue should support Deep Navy rather than compete with it.

Use it for:

- Secondary sections
- Cards
- Supporting backgrounds
- Event graphics
- Highlighted content
- Secondary buttons
- Visual blocks

Royal Blue can become more prominent in event-specific branding while remaining connected to the master brand.

---

# 6. Accent Colors

Mid Blue and Muted Blue should generally appear in smaller quantities.

Use them for:

- Icons
- Links
- Borders
- Charts
- Illustrations
- Data visualization
- Decorative elements
- Active states
- Secondary highlights

Avoid using the accent colors as the dominant color of the entire brand system.

---

# 7. Warm Paper Usage

Warm Paper is an important part of the KaizenHacks visual identity because it introduces warmth into the blue palette.

Use it selectively for:

- Editorial layouts
- Posters
- Printed documents
- Campaign graphics
- Cards
- Photography frames
- Backgrounds
- Brand storytelling

Warm Paper should feel intentional.

Do not use it as a generic beige replacement across every design.

---

# 8. Off White Usage

Off White should be the default light neutral.

Use it for:

- Light backgrounds
- Documents
- Presentations
- Website sections
- Cards
- Social graphics
- Negative space

When a design does not require a strong white background, Off White should generally be preferred over pure white.

---

# 9. Ink Usage

Ink is primarily a structural color.

Use it for:

- Body text
- Fine print
- Dark backgrounds
- Footer areas
- Overlays
- High-contrast elements

Do not automatically use Ink wherever Deep Navy would be appropriate.

Deep Navy represents the brand.

Ink represents the neutral structure around it.

---

# 10. Recommended Combinations

## Combination A — Core Brand

**Deep Navy + Off White + Royal Blue**

Use for:

- Main brand materials
- Presentations
- Website
- Corporate/organizational communication

This should be the safest default combination.

---

## Combination B — Editorial

**Ink + Warm Paper + Deep Navy + Royal Blue**

Use for:

- Posters
- Editorial campaigns
- Printed materials
- Brand storytelling

This combination emphasizes the intellectual/editorial side of KaizenHacks.

---

## Combination C — Light

**Off White + Deep Navy + Mid Blue**

Use for:

- Documents
- Information-heavy layouts
- Websites
- Presentations

---

## Combination D — Dark

**Ink + Deep Navy + Off White + Muted Blue**

Use for:

- Dark interfaces
- Technology-focused materials
- Presentations
- Digital campaigns

---

## Combination E — Event

**Deep Navy + Royal Blue + Mid Blue + Off White**

Use for:

- Hackathons
- Competitions
- Workshops
- Conferences
- Event campaigns

---

# 11. Color Proportion

As a general starting point, designs should be weighted toward neutrals and primary colors rather than using every palette color equally.

A useful starting relationship is:

- **50–70%** primary/neutral background
- **20–35%** secondary/supporting color
- **5–15%** accent color

These are guidelines rather than fixed mathematical requirements.

The more colors a composition contains, the more restrained their individual usage should become.

---

# 12. Avoid Rainbow Usage

Do not use all seven brand colors simply because they are available.

A strong composition may use only:

- Deep Navy + Off White
- Deep Navy + Warm Paper
- Deep Navy + Royal Blue
- Ink + Warm Paper + Royal Blue

Using fewer colors generally produces a stronger and more recognizable identity.

---

# 13. Background Rules

### Dark Background

Preferred:

- Deep Navy
- Ink
- Royal Blue in selected cases

Use:

- Off White
- Warm Paper
- Muted Blue

for supporting elements where contrast permits.

### Light Background

Preferred:

- Off White
- Warm Paper

Use:

- Deep Navy
- Ink
- Royal Blue

for text and major elements.

---

# 14. Text and Contrast

Text must remain readable against its background.

Always check:

- Contrast
- Font size
- Font weight
- Image interference
- Screen conditions
- Print conditions

Avoid placing:

- Mid Blue text on Mid Blue backgrounds
- Muted Blue text on light blue backgrounds
- Low-weight text on busy imagery
- Warm Paper text on light backgrounds with insufficient contrast

When in doubt, use **Deep Navy or Ink for dark text** and **Off White for light text**.

---

# 15. Color on Photography

When typography or graphics are placed over photography:

1. Check the image's natural contrast.
2. Avoid placing text over visually busy areas.
3. Use Deep Navy, Ink, or Off White where appropriate.
4. Add a controlled overlay when necessary.
5. Do not force brand colors onto an image if doing so harms readability.

Brand consistency should never come at the expense of legibility.

---

# 16. Gradients

Gradients may be used, but should remain subtle and controlled.

Preferred gradient relationships should generally remain within the blue family.

Examples:

**Deep Navy → Royal Blue**

**Royal Blue → Mid Blue**

**Deep Navy → Muted Blue**

Avoid:

- Neon gradients
- Rainbow gradients
- Excessive glow effects
- Unrelated colors
- Highly saturated cyberpunk-style gradients

The brand should remain professional and editorial rather than overly futuristic.

---

# 17. Event Color Adaptation

Individual KaizenHacks events may emphasize different colors from the master palette.

For example:

- A technology competition may emphasize Royal Blue and Mid Blue.
- A conference may emphasize Deep Navy and Warm Paper.
- A community initiative may use more Off White and Muted Blue.

However, event palettes should normally remain connected to the master KaizenHacks palette.

Do not create completely unrelated colors unless an event is intentionally established as an independent sub-brand.

---

# 18. Accessibility

Color must never be the only method of communicating information.

For example, do not communicate:

- Success only through green
- Failure only through red
- Categories only through different colors

Support color with:

- Text
- Icons
- Labels
- Patterns
- Shapes
- Position
- Other visual cues

Always verify sufficient contrast for important text and interactive elements.

---

# 19. Color and Typography

Typography and color should work together.

### Major headlines

Prefer:

- Deep Navy
- Ink
- Off White
- Warm Paper where appropriate

### Body text

Prefer:

- Ink
- Deep Navy

### Supporting information

May use:

- Royal Blue
- Mid Blue

provided sufficient contrast is maintained.

Do not use bright or low-contrast accent colors for large amounts of body text.

---

# 20. Do Not

Avoid:

- Making every element blue
- Using every palette color simultaneously
- Replacing Deep Navy with random dark blues
- Introducing unrelated neon colors
- Using gradients excessively
- Using poor-contrast text
- Using Warm Paper as a generic beige everywhere
- Using accent blues as the primary brand color
- Creating event palettes completely disconnected from KaizenHacks
- Using color effects that overpower typography or imagery

---

# 21. Brand Color Test

Before approving a design, ask:

1. Is the KaizenHacks identity recognizable without the logo?
2. Is Deep Navy still clearly the primary brand color?
3. Are secondary and accent colors supporting rather than competing?
4. Is there enough neutral space?
5. Is the contrast accessible?
6. Does the color combination feel professional?
7. Does it feel technological without becoming overly futuristic?
8. Does it retain the editorial character of the brand?
9. Is every color serving a purpose?

If the answer to several of these is no, simplify the palette.

## Final Rule

**Use color to establish hierarchy, not decoration.**

KaizenHacks should be recognizable through the disciplined relationship between its deep navy foundation, blue spectrum, warm paper neutral, and restrained light/dark neutrals.
